# GPS-Based Safety Wearables: Research Design Prototype

A mixed-methods research project on how GPS-based watches and necklaces can help address safety concerns for women, children and mentally challenged individuals in Pakistan.

**Live demo:** https://Taniya-Tariq.github.io/gps-safety-research-prototype/

## About the Project

Safety concerns such as harassment and abduction affect women, children and vulnerable individuals. This project studies whether wearable GPS devices, with features like real-time location sharing, one-touch SOS alerts and safe-zone notifications, can help, and what users think about them.

## What is in this Repository

- `index.html`: an interactive page with:
  - the research design flow
  - research questions
  - methods and sample
  - a virtual device simulation with a feedback form
  - pilot evidence
  - ethics and limitations

## Research Approach

This is a mixed-methods study:

- **Quantitative:** a survey (Google Forms) sent to participants across several cities, including Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi
- **Qualitative:** in-depth interviews and group discussions on privacy, trust and design preferences
- **Prototype and pilot test:** a working GPS device (ESP32, SIM808, SIM800L) tested with users, who rated ease of use, accuracy, design and battery life
- **Virtual prototype:** an online simulation of the watch and necklace with a guardian app, used for further testing

## Key Findings (from the group's research)

- Strong demand for real-time location sharing and emergency access
- Privacy of location data is a major concern
- Users prefer small, discreet wearable devices

## Ethics

- Guardian consent is needed for children and mentally challenged participants
- Location data must be kept private and secure
- No participant personal data is stored in this repository
